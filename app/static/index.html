Two readings of every answer

The model answers. Then detectors estimate the chance that answer is wrong in two ways: from its words (how sure it sounded) and from its inside (its hidden state). When the words sound sure but the inside doesn't, the model is bluffing. The guard blocks any answer whose deciding reading crosses the dashed line.

How a result is made

  1. Questions1,448 general trivia questions, and 326 Kenya items written by four students and checked: 211 real Kenya questions, 82 made-up ones about things that do not exist, and 33 matched UK questions.
  2. The model answers, four timesQwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct, an open model small enough to run on a free GPU, answers each question once normally and three more times with "I don't know" offered as an option.
  3. Its hidden state is recordedAt the end of each answer, the 1,536 numbers inside layers 21 and 25 of its 28 layers: what it "thinks" at that moment.
  4. Each answer is marked right or wrongAgainst the accepted answers, with each question's spelling variants.
  5. Four detectors are trainedOn trivia only. Two read the words' confidence (logistic regression, XGBoost); two read the hidden state (a linear probe and a small neural network, the MLP probe).
  6. They are tested on questions they never sawHeld-out trivia, and every Kenya item. Metrics come with 95% confidence intervals.
  7. The best one guards a live APIRegistered as the champion and served by this page's API, which blocks an answer when the deciding detector's chance of it being wrong is above 25%. On the Ask tab you can let any of the four decide.

Four versions of the model

The same questions and detectors, with one change to the model each time. Each version's detectors are trained on its own answers.

Words used on this page

Chance it's wrong, P(wrong)
A detector's estimate that an answer is wrong, from 0% (surely right) to 100% (surely wrong).
Its words
What the two outside detectors (logistic regression and XGBoost) conclude from how sure the model was of each word it wrote (8 numbers per answer).
Its inside
What the probes conclude from the model's hidden state: 1,536 numbers from inside layer 21.
The guard's line
25%. A confident wrong answer is counted as three times worse than an unnecessary refusal, so refusing pays off once the chance of being wrong passes 1 ÷ (1 + 3).
Bluff
A wrong answer stated with confidence. Bluff caught: the words sounded sure, but the inside reading crossed the guard's line.
Silent knowing
Of the wrong answers the model gave without saying "I don't know", the share where its hidden state was already flagged as unsure (hypothesis H1).
AUROC
Take one right and one wrong answer at random: how often the detector gives the wrong one the higher chance of being wrong. 0.5 is a coin flip; 1.0 is perfect.
PR-AUC
Like AUROC, but focused on finding the wrong answers: the average precision when the detector's most suspicious answers are checked first.
ECE (calibration error)
How far a detector's chances are from reality on average: 0.10 means its "60%" answers are wrong about 50% or 70% of the time.
Wrong at 80% coverage
If the system answered only the 80% of questions the detector is surest about, the share of those answers that would still be wrong.
Knowing vs saying
Knowing: how well the hidden state separates right from wrong answers (internal AUROC). Saying: how well the model's own "I don't know" does (expressed AUROC). The difference is the verbalisation gap.
95% confidence interval
The range the true value very likely lies in, given how many test questions there were. Shown as whiskers on the charts.
RAG
Retrieval-augmented generation: three relevant passages from Kenyan documents are pasted in front of the question.
QLoRA
A cheap way to fine-tune: the model is frozen (and compressed to 4 bits while training) and only a small add-on of 18.5 million weights is trained.
