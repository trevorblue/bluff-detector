Bluff Detector: live guard

A small language model (Qwen2.5-1.5B) answers your question. A detector then reads the model's own hidden state to estimate the chance the answer is wrong, and a guard refuses to answer when that chance is too high. The point: catch the model when it bluffs, sounding sure while its inside is not.

1The model answersIt writes a short answer, word by word, and the API records how sure it was of each word and its hidden state.
2Detectors read itFour trained detectors each estimate P(wrong): two from the word confidences, two from the hidden state.
3The guard decidesIf the served detector's P(wrong) is above the line, the answer is withheld; otherwise it is shown.

Glossary: what the words on this page mean
P(wrong)
A detector's estimate of the chance that the model's answer is wrong, from 0 (surely right) to 1 (surely wrong).
The guard's line (t_abstain)
The guard withholds an answer when P(wrong) is above it. Here a confident wrong answer counts as 3 times worse than a needless refusal, so the line is 1 / (1 + 3) = 0.25.
Hidden state
The 1,536 numbers inside the model at the end of its answer (layer 21 of 28): its internal "thought". The probes read it.
Outside vs inside
Outside detectors (logistic regression, XGBoost) only see how sure the model was of its words. Inside detectors (the probes) read the hidden state.
Silent knowing
The model states a wrong answer confidently while its hidden state already signals doubt. Hypothesis H1 of the project.
AUROC
Take one right and one wrong answer at random: the share of times the detector gives the wrong one the higher P(wrong). 1.0 = perfect, 0.5 = a coin flip.
ECE (calibration error)
How far P(wrong) is from the real share of wrong answers, on average. 0 = its probabilities can be taken at face value.
Entropy ("hesitation")
How spread the model's probability was over the possible next words. 0 = no doubt at all; higher = torn between options.
95% CI
The range the true value very likely lies in, given how many test questions there were.
